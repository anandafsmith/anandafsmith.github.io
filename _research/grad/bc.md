Did you know that baryons are not cold dark matter (CDM)?
We clever cosmologists already know this, but whether this distinction is important for ongoing spectroscopic galaxy surveys is a more pressing question.
Acoustic waves in the primordial baryon-photon plasma imprint large-scale relative baryon-CDM density and velocity perturbations after recombination; if these are not correctly accounted for, they could bias cosmological information extracted from galaxy clustering data!
With [Misha Ivanov](https://physics.mit.edu/faculty/mikhail-ivanov/), I systematically incorporated these relative in the leading theoretical treatment of galaxies as biased tracers.
We fit our model to the galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum measurements from data release 1 (DR1) of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI).
Anticlimactically, relative baryon-CDM perturbations seem to have basically no effect on the cosmology inferred from the data; in particular, their inclusion does not alleviate the apparent preference for dynamical dark energy (as you can see from the contour to the right).
